Start here: the reassuring part

Your website will not change at all. Not one line. Your domain stays white-box.co.il, your email addresses stay exactly the same, and the 7 places info@white-box.co.il appears in the site's code stay exactly as they are. This migration changes only where your mail is delivered.

Your contact form does not send through the cPanel mail server, so nothing below can break the form itself. One nuance: the form posts to a service called Web3Forms, which emails a notification to both info@ and daphna@. That notification is delivered to the two mailboxes you are migrating — so it follows your MX record like any other incoming mail, and works normally once mail is at Google. Step 7 has you test exactly this.

Why this migration matters more than it looks

A reverse lookup of the mail server 148.251.82.238 returns cp57.box.co.il. Your mail host is BOX — the provider you already left because it was terrible. The websites moved; the email never followed, and has been sitting there ever since. That is the most likely reason it "fails or disconnects often." This is not a new decision — it is finishing one you already made.

The one rule

The order of the ten steps is the safety mechanism. Your old mail gets copied across while email is still flowing normally to cPanel. By the time you flip the switch, nothing is at risk — and everything stays reversible until the very last step.

The five words you need to understand

TermWhat it actually means
DNSThe phone book of the internet. It translates white-box.co.il into the address of an actual computer. Yours is managed at Netlify.
MX recordThe line in the phone book that says “send this domain's email to this server.” This is the switch you are flipping.
SPFA public note saying “only these servers may send email as me.” Stops others forging your address, and keeps your mail out of spam folders.
DKIMAn invisible signature on every email you send, proving it really came from you and was not tampered with.
DMARCYour instruction to other mail servers about what to do when an email fails the SPF and DKIM checks.
TTL“Time To Live” — how long the world may remember an old answer before asking again. Lower means your changes take effect faster.

Where you will be clicking

Two websites, and it is worth being clear which is which, because it is easy to get lost:

  • Netlify — app.netlify.com → Domains → white-box.co.il → DNS records. Every DNS change below happens here. Your DNS lives at Netlify even though your email does not.
  • Google Admin — admin.google.com. Where you create mailboxes and get your DKIM key.

You will never log into cPanel again, except to check old mail and — at the very end — to cancel it.

The order of operations

Read this list before doing anything.

  1. Lower the TTL — a day ahead, so the switch happens fast
  2. Sign up for Google Workspace and verify you own the domain
  3. Create your mailboxes
  4. Copy all your old mail across — while email still flows to cPanel
  5. Flip the MX record — this is the actual switch
  6. Fix the SPF record
  7. Test thoroughly
  8. Run the mail copy one final time, to catch the gap
  9. Add DKIM and DMARC
  10. Wait two weeks, then cancel cPanel

1Lower the TTL — do this the day before

Why: TTL is how long the internet caches the old answer. Yours is probably 1 hour. Lower it to 5 minutes first, and tomorrow's switch takes effect in 5 minutes instead of an hour. A small step that removes a lot of anxiety later.

  1. Netlify → Domains → white-box.co.il → DNS records
  2. Find the MX record pointing at mail.white-box.co.il
  3. Edit it and change TTL to 300 (300 seconds = 5 minutes)
  4. Save. Change nothing else.

Then wait a day before Step 5. You can do Steps 2–4 during that wait.

2Sign up and verify the domain

Go to workspace.google.com and sign up for Business Starter.

  • When it asks for your domain, enter white-box.co.il
  • Make your first account daphna@white-box.co.il — see Step 3 for why

Google then asks you to prove you own the domain, usually by adding a TXT record.

Possible shortcut

Your site already carries a Google Search Console verification tag. Google sometimes recognises this and skips verification entirely. If it offers, take it.

If you do need to add the record:

  1. Netlify → DNS records → Add new record
  2. Type: TXT
  3. Name: leave empty. Netlify may show this as @ or as the bare domain — all three mean “the domain itself”
  4. Value: paste exactly what Google gives you (it starts with google-site-verification=)
  5. Save, then click verify in Google. Usually works within minutes.
Do not tidy up

You now have two TXT records on the domain — your existing SPF, and this new verification one. That is correct and expected. Do not delete the SPF one to “make room.” Multiple TXT records are completely normal.

3Create your mailboxes — and save money

You have two addresses, but you probably only need one paid account.

  • daphna@white-box.co.il — your real user account. This is the one you pay for.
  • info@white-box.co.il — add as an alias on that same account.

An alias is free. Mail to info@ lands in the same inbox, and you can still send as info@ when you want to appear as the studio rather than yourself. That is roughly 27 ILS per month instead of 54.

To set it up: Admin → Directory → Users → click your account → User information → Email aliases → add info.

Only make info@ a separate paid account if someone other than you needs their own private login for it.

4Copy your old mail across — the important one

Do this while email is still being delivered to cPanel. You are copying, not moving. Nothing is deleted from cPanel, so there is no way to lose anything at this step.

Google Admin → Data migration. If you cannot find it, type “data migration” into the admin search bar.

Run it for both info@ and daphna@ if they are separate mailboxes on cPanel today.

Depending on how many years of mail you have, this takes anywhere from minutes to several hours. Let it finish. Then open Gmail and actually look — confirm old messages are really there and folders came across — before going anywhere near Step 5.

Port 993 was verified as open and reachable on your mail server, so this connection should work.

5Flip the MX record — the actual switch

This is the moment new mail starts going to Google. With TTL at 300, it takes about 5 minutes.

  1. Netlify → DNS records
  2. Delete the existing MX record — mail.white-box.co.il, priority 0
  3. Add new record, with these values:
Type: MX
Name: (leave empty — the domain itself)
Value: smtp.google.com
Priority: 1
TTL: 300

That single record is all Google needs — they simplified this in 2023. If you find an older guide listing five ASPMX.L.GOOGLE.COM records, that is the legacy setup. It still works, but you do not need it.

Timing

Do this on a weekday morning, not a Friday afternoon — so if anything looks wrong, you have the whole day to sort it out.

6Fix the SPF record

Do this straight after the MX change. If you skip it, mail you send from Gmail may land in recipients' spam folders.

  1. Netlify → DNS records → find the TXT record whose value starts with v=spf1
  2. Edit it. Do not add a second one — see the mistakes section
  3. Replace the value with:
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ip4:148.251.82.238 ~all

This temporarily authorises both Google and your old server, which is the safe choice while both still exist.

Later, once cPanel is cancelled in Step 10, simplify it to:

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

7Test

Do not skip this, and do not test in only one direction.

  • Send an email from Gmail to your personal Gmail or other address — it arrives, and is not in spam
  • Send an email from that outside address to info@white-box.co.il — it arrives in Gmail
  • Repeat the incoming test for daphna@white-box.co.il
  • Submit the contact form on white-box.co.il and confirm the notification still reaches you
  • Reply to a message and confirm the reply sends

You can check the DNS side at mxtoolbox.com — enter white-box.co.il, and MX should show smtp.google.com.

8Run the migration once more

Between your Step 4 copy and the Step 5 switch, a few emails may have landed on cPanel. Re-run the same Data Migration from Step 4. It skips everything already copied and picks up only the stragglers.

9Add DKIM and DMARC

These are not strictly required for mail to work, but they are what keeps you out of spam folders long term. Do them a few days after everything else is stable.

DKIM

  1. Google Admin → Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email
  2. Click Generate new record — use 2048-bit
  3. Google shows you a name (google._domainkey) and a very long value
  4. In Netlify: Add new record → Type TXT → Name google._domainkey → Value: the long string, pasted exactly
  5. Wait about 15 minutes, return to Google, click Start authentication

Copy that long value carefully. A single missing character breaks it silently.

DMARC

Add one more TXT record in Netlify:

Name: _dmarc
Value: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:daphna@white-box.co.il

p=none means “monitor only, block nothing.” This is deliberate: you get reports with zero risk of legitimate mail being rejected. Only consider tightening to p=quarantine after months of clean reports, and there is no urgency at all.

Never do this

Do not start with p=reject. It can silently destroy real email.

10Cancel cPanel

Wait a full two weeks after Step 7 passes. Not two days. You want certainty that nothing was missed — including monthly senders such as invoices, or suppliers who email you only occasionally.

Once you are confident:

  1. Do one last check of the cPanel webmail for anything unexpected
  2. Simplify SPF to v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all — drop the old server's IP
  3. Raise TTLs back to 3600. You no longer need fast changes
  4. Cancel the cPanel account
The bonus

Those “AutoSSL reduced coverage” warning emails stop forever. That whole problem existed only because the cPanel server was trying to manage certificates for a website it does not host. Once mail leaves, the account has no reason to exist.

Four mistakes that break email

Worth reading twice.

  1. Two SPF records. You must have exactly one TXT record starting with v=spf1. Two is worse than none — mail servers see the conflict and fail the check entirely. In Step 6, edit the existing record. Never add a second.
  2. Deleting the wrong TXT record. After Step 2 you will have several TXT records on the domain: SPF, Google verification, and later DKIM and DMARC. They coexist happily. Deleting one to tidy up breaks whatever depended on it.
  3. Switching MX before copying mail. Flip MX first and new mail arrives in an empty Google mailbox while your history sits stranded on a server you are about to cancel. Copy first. Always.
  4. Cancelling cPanel too early. A few more weeks of hosting fees is nothing compared to discovering in October that a client's email from September went to a server that no longer exists.

If something goes wrong

You can always undo the switch. Nothing here is permanent until you cancel cPanel in Step 10 — which is exactly why Step 10 is last.

To roll back: in Netlify, delete the smtp.google.com MX record and re-create the original one. With TTL at 300, mail flows back to cPanel within about 5 minutes, exactly as before.
