Findings

The pattern behind findings 1 and 2

Two of your control panels display urgent-sounding warnings that a domain "isn't pointing at us." In both cases the domain is pointing exactly where it should, and the correct action is to change nothing. A hosting panel can only see whether it is being used — it cannot know you deliberately host elsewhere. Treat "connect your domain to us" prompts as questions, never instructions.

Ask1  uPress holds an unused white-box.co.il entry — never connect it

The uPress dashboard shows a www.white-box.co.il site stuck at "ממתין לחיבור דומיין" (waiting for domain connection), created around June 2026 and never connected. uPress is asking you to point the domain at its servers.

Do not do this. Your nameservers are the master switch for the whole domain — website, the MX record that delivers your mail, SPF, and the accessibility-page redirects all live in the Netlify DNS zone. Repointing them at uPress would hand control to a server holding none of those records: the site would go down and mail would bounce. It could not work in any case — uPress is managed WordPress hosting and white-box.co.il is a hand-built static site.

What to ask uPress support: "is the white-box.co.il site in my package billed separately, or is it just using one of my site slots?" If it is only a slot, removing it costs nothing, frees the slot, and stops the warning permanently.

Act2  Your email is still on BOX — the host you already left

A reverse lookup of the mail server 148.251.82.238 returns cp57.box.co.il. The flaky cPanel account is BOX — the provider you moved away from because it was terrible.

The websites were moved. The email never followed, and has been sitting there ever since. That is the most likely explanation for the failures and disconnections you have been experiencing: it is the same service, still delivering your business enquiries.

This does not change the migration plan, but it does change its urgency. You already made this decision once — the mail simply did not come with you. Runbook: docs/email-migration-plan.md and docs/email-migration-plan.pdf.

Resolved3  Web3Forms is now on Pro

This audit originally flagged the free plan's 250 submissions per month as the only free service in the inventory that could quietly run out. The account has since moved to Pro, with a 10,000/month allowance, so the ceiling is no longer a practical concern.

Corrected 1 September 2026: the earlier claim that notifications are copied to both info@ and daphna@ was wrong. The form's markup carried a cc field, but Web3Forms has no such field — it was passed through as ordinary form data and copied the notification to nobody. The only configured recipient is daphna.rhone@gmail.com, set in the Web3Forms dashboard. The dead field has been removed from the markup; recipients are managed in the dashboard under Settings > Email Configuration (up to 5, plus CC/BCC on Pro).

This also simplifies the email migration: form notifications go to a Gmail address, so they never touch the MX record being changed and cannot be affected by the cutover.

The answer is that Netlify hosts a second domain, hydronova-robotics.com (confirmed live on Netlify). The account was on the free plan until that site was added. Justified; no action.

Context5  You already use the uPress email service you were evaluating

white-box-holy.com runs on uPress with its mail on inbox.co.il — the same service uPress would supply for white-box.co.il. You therefore have direct experience of it, which is better evidence than any feature page.

This does not change the Google Workspace recommendation — the 500MB-per-mailbox cap and the principle of keeping mail independent of web hosting both still stand — but the judgement is yours, with information the recommendation did not have.

Note6  GSAP is free, despite what older guides say

ScrollTrigger used to be a paid Club GreenSock plugin. Webflow acquired GSAP and made the entire library, ScrollTrigger included, free for commercial use in April 2025. No licence is owed. Much of the advice still online predates that change.

Opportunity7  The second mail bill may be removable — decide before you set Workspace up

You pay two separate mail providers: BOX for white-box.co.il and inbox.co.il for white-box-holy.com. Google Workspace can hold several domains on one account, and the cost depends entirely on which of two modes you choose:

  • Domain alias — free, no extra licence. Mail to anything@white-box-holy.com lands in the matching @white-box.co.il inbox, and you can still send as either address. If you simply want Holy enquiries reaching you, this costs nothing and inbox.co.il could be cancelled outright.
  • Secondary domain — one licence per mailbox (~27 ILS/mo each). Needed only if Holy mail must live in a genuinely separate account, e.g. someone else handles it.

Check this before cancelling anything: does the WooCommerce store send its order confirmations through inbox.co.il? The SPF record for white-box-holy.com authorises the uPress server directly (ip4:185.108.148.247), which suggests the store sends its own mail and would be unaffected. Confirm in WordPress admin — look for an SMTP plugin and see which host it points at. If it does route through inbox.co.il, that must be re-pointed before cancelling, or customers stop receiving order emails.

Why the timing matters: adding a domain at Workspace setup is a few clicks. Doing it after everything is running is a second migration. This decision is much cheaper to make now.

Note8  Neither domain has DKIM or DMARC

white-box.co.il has neither, and white-box-holy.com has neither either — no DKIM selector responds and there is no _dmarc record on either domain. These are the records that keep your mail out of recipients' spam folders and stop others forging your addresses.

Step 9 of the email runbook adds both for white-box.co.il. Worth doing the same for the Holy domain, which matters more for a shop sending order confirmations to customers.
